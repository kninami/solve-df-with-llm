# HD-wallet-aware local blockchain query for cryptocurrency address derivation

## Summary

Cryptocurrency transaction forensic examinations must guarantee completeness, confidentiality, and integrity, but many publicly available Bitcoin query tools fail to find transactions generated by Hierarchical Deterministic wallets due to failures in their address-derivation methods, and often rely on third-party servers that risk disclosing the existence or scope of an investigation. BlockQuery is designed from a forensic standpoint to meet four criteria: open source, confidential (no third-party data provider), automatic conversion between key representations, and manual adjustment of derivation depth.

## Details

Operating on a local copy of the blockchain rather than querying third-party servers, BlockQuery derives Legacy, Native SegWit, and Nested SegWit addresses from a provided extended public/private key, and allows the investigator to manually extend the derivation index range beyond the standard address-gap-limit default (commonly 20), which is necessary to discover transactions in wallets with unusually high derivation gaps or "address islands" that gap-limit-respecting tools would miss.

## Examples

- In a comparative evaluation, only one tool other than BlockQuery (Ledger's xpub-scan) was able to find all transactions generated by the provided extended keys, because it derived addresses across all three address formats regardless of the key format supplied in the query; every other compared tool failed to find any transactions from wallets with high derivation gaps.
