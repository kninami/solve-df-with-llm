# Train frontline investigators on digital forensic unit capabilities and let DFU practitioners take a proactive scene-attendance role

## Summary

Reduce reliance on individual investigators' informal technical awareness for deciding whether to involve a digital forensic unit (DFU) by systematically training frontline investigators on what digital traces exist and what a DFU can recover, and by giving DFU practitioners latitude to proactively attend or flag cases they judge relevant even when not formally requested.

## Addresses

- [[weaknesses/Investigators unfamiliar with digital forensic unit capabilities under-request specialist involvement in cases with relevant digital evidence]]

## How To Apply

Provide regular training and informal briefing sessions for frontline officers and investigators covering the range of digital traces a DFU can recover and the case indicators (e.g. suspected cryptocurrency, darknet, or social-media involvement) that should prompt a referral, rather than relying on individual officers to develop this awareness informally over time. Where staffing allows, give DFU practitioners some latitude to proactively attend or monitor cases they judge may benefit from digital forensic input, mirroring the observed instance where a DFU member's own initiative (visiting a scene despite not being formally called) recovered evidence (an SD card) that would otherwise have been missed. Periodically review case outcomes for evidence that a case handled without DFU involvement later turned out to have relevant digital evidence, to identify recurring referral gaps and refine training accordingly.
