# Frame at least one hypothesis as an innocence hypothesis and visualize argument structure when applying an argument-based evaluative-opinion method

## Summary

When using an argument-based method to produce an evaluative opinion, deliberately include an innocence-oriented (not just an alternative guilt) hypothesis to avoid a fairness-undermining outcome, and use a visual representation of the argument's components and attacks once the evidence set grows large enough to risk losing oversight of the reasoning.

## Addresses

- [[weaknesses/Argument-based evaluative-opinion method loses oversight and risks bias if hypotheses are not framed with an innocence option]]

## How To Apply

At the hypothesis-framing step, explicitly check that at least one of the two compared hypotheses represents innocence with respect to the ultimate investigative question, rather than two variants of a guilt theory. As the number of grounds, inferential leaps, and attacks under a hypothesis grows, maintain a visual diagram of the argument structure (components and attacks) alongside the Argument Matrix to avoid losing track of dependencies between grounds.
