# Train prosecutors and investigators on the Article 32 lawful-access exceptions and cross-border cooperation channels before cloud evidence acquisition

## Summary

Provide dedicated training on international cooperation and cloud computing technology to prosecutors, judicial staff, and investigators so that decisions to acquire cross-border cloud evidence are grounded in the correct legal basis (mutual legal assistance, a European Investigation Order, or one of the two Article 32 direct-access exceptions) from the outset, rather than defaulting to directing police to remotely secure foreign-hosted data without legal authority to do so.

## Addresses

- [[weaknesses/Investigators and prosecutors misunderstand the legal basis for remote cross-border access to cloud-stored data]]

## How To Apply

Require training on cross-border digital evidence collection and cloud computing technology as part of standard prosecutorial and judicial professional development, covering specifically: which formal cooperation instruments are available and how to invoke them, the precise scope of the two Article 32 direct-access exceptions (and that neither is satisfied merely by a web interface being reachable), and how and when to use the Cybercrime Convention's 24/7 contact-point network for urgent preservation or technical consultation. Before commissioning police or technical staff to secure data from a foreign server, require an explicit documented determination of which legal basis applies, and escalate to a formal mutual legal assistance or European Investigation Order request by default when no Article 32 exception clearly applies.
