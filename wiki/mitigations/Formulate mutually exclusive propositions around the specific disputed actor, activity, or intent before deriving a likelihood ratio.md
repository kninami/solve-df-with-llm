# Formulate mutually exclusive propositions around the specific disputed actor, activity, or intent before deriving a likelihood ratio

## Summary

Before computing a likelihood ratio, explicitly identify which of the three dispute types (disputed actor, disputed activity, or disputed intent) is actually at issue between the parties, and build the proposition pair to isolate exactly that dispute, checking that the two propositions are mutually exclusive and that undisputed case information (e.g. an agreed time period or an agreed suspect-device link) is left out of the propositions rather than redundantly restated.

## Addresses

- [[weaknesses/Propositions translated directly from disputed explanations can yield an uninformative likelihood ratio]]

## How To Apply

Work through the disputed-actor/disputed-activity/disputed-intent classification before drafting propositions, and pressure-test a draft pair by checking whether the evidence's probability genuinely differs between them — if it does not (as when both propositions merely restate the undisputed presence of the material), revise the proposition to name the specific alleged activity, actor, or awareness state actually contested. Where the suspect offers no comment or an ambiguous account, use one of the documented fallback strategies (collaboratively list possible explanations with both parties, evaluate multiple candidate proposition sets, or report against a background-prevalence explanation) rather than defaulting to an unrefined proposition pair.
