# Apply the manual examination procedure's staged viability assessment and escalate to formal forensic extraction when needed

## Summary

Before conducting a device manual examination, work through the seven-stage manual examination procedure (MEP) to confirm relevant data can be identified, gathered, captured, and evidenced without unacceptable compromise, and default to formal forensic extraction whenever any stage cannot be positively answered.

## Addresses

- [[weaknesses/Manual on-device examination alters device state and cannot capture non-visible sub-surface data]]

## How To Apply

At scene, run through the MEP's seven staged questions before touching a device: identify what data is relevant and whether it is likely to be visible surface-level content or non-visible sub-surface content; confirm all relevant data can practically be gathered manually; assess whether navigation can proceed without compromising the data (and whether the practitioner is sufficiently competent to minimize handling errors); check whether data authenticity is in question; confirm adequate capture equipment (e.g. a sanitized camera) is available; plan how captured content will enter the evidence chain with integrity preserved; and weigh whether a manual examination is proportionate given the inquiry. If sub-surface data is likely to be relevant, authenticity is in doubt, or any other stage cannot be satisfied, submit the device for formal forensic extraction instead of, or in addition to, the manual examination.
