# Map each validation standard's terminology to a single internally consistent glossary before applying multiple standards together

## Summary

Before applying more than one digital forensic method-validation standard or guidance document (e.g. ISO/IEC 17025, ISO/IEC 27041, ISO/IEC 27042, ILAC G19, or a national regulator's codes of practice) together in an organization's validation process, explicitly map each document's specific terminology (particularly "verification," "customer," and "end-user") into a single internally consistent glossary, so staff apply a shared, unambiguous meaning rather than the term's meaning in whichever specific document they happen to be reading.

## Addresses

- [[weaknesses/Overloaded and inconsistent terminology across digital forensic validation standards causes confusion in method validation]]

## How To Apply

Before or during adoption of a validation process that draws on multiple standards, have a qualified reviewer identify every term used with a materially different meaning across the applicable documents, and produce an internal glossary mapping each term to the specific meaning the organization intends when it uses that term internally, cross-referencing which source standard's definition it corresponds to. In particular, explicitly define "customer" and "end-user" broadly enough to include the full range of parties who might reasonably be considered end-users of a forensic result (not only the criminal justice system as the ultimate consumer, but also the instructing law-enforcement or prosecutorial body that commissions the work), and review whether the organization's own instruction-taking and requirements-gathering process is inadvertently skewed toward one interpretation, creating confirmation-bias risk. Revisit the glossary whenever an applicable standard is revised.
