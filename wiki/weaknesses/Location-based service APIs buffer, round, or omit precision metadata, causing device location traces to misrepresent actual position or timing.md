# Location-based service APIs buffer, round, or omit precision metadata, causing device location traces to misrepresent actual position or timing

## Summary

Documented cases include an AirTag location update buffered during an airplane-mode period and only transmitted roughly 1,734 km and several hours later than the position was actually measured; a database field using the sentinel value -1 to represent an unknown or intentionally coarse accuracy/area rather than a genuine precise reading; and forensic tool visualizations that round displayed coordinates to fewer decimal places than the underlying source data contains, or omit an available accuracy-radius field entirely. Each represents a distinct mechanism by which the trace an investigator ultimately examines can diverge, in position, in timing, or in apparent precision, from what the device actually recorded.

## Why It Matters

An investigator who treats a recovered location trace's timestamp as the moment of actual measurement, or its coordinate as precise to the number of decimal places shown, risks a materially wrong conclusion about where a device (and its likely carrier) was and when -- as starkly illustrated by the AirTac buffering case, where a naive reading would place the tracked object roughly 1,734 km from its actual location at the recorded time. Because these failure modes span multiple distinct stages (device-side buffering, database-encoding conventions, and forensic-tool display choices), no single check catches all of them, and an investigator unaware of a specific mechanism has no way to know it applies to the trace in front of them.
