# DFR maturity assessment model structure was validated with only 10 practitioners and lacks practical field testing

## Summary

The authors state directly that "the limitation of this study is that only 10 participants were interviewed, as such it is possible that not all the aspects of DFR were revealed," specifically noting that clarity on the various roles within DFR and the implications of collecting private information during a readiness assessment did not emerge from the study, and that "the models still require further testing in practice before they can be generalised."

## Why It Matters

An organization adopting this DFRCF/DFMM structure to self-assess its forensic readiness maturity is relying on a domain structure shaped by a small, non-exhaustive practitioner sample rather than a large-scale validated instrument — gaps in role definitions or unaddressed privacy implications of the assessment process itself may not surface until the organization is already partway through applying the model.
