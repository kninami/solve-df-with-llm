# LLM-generated synthetic mobile forensic dataset content can be factually incorrect or incomplete without expert validation

## Summary

The authors state plainly that "generative AI can produce social prejudices or convincing-sounding but factually incorrect information" and that "the data generated by AI is not simply adopted. Instead, it must first be checked for completeness and plausibility, involving experienced forensic professionals." The toolchain's automated validation is limited to detecting syntactic errors in the generated storyboard; it does not verify factual or contextual plausibility.

## Why It Matters

A synthetic dataset intended for training or validating forensic tools that contains unreviewed factually-incorrect or implausible content risks teaching or validating those tools against unrealistic ground truth, undermining the dataset's purpose. Because the paper's own evaluation of human ability to distinguish AI-generated from authentic data was limited (a small, non-rigorous field trial, not a controlled human assessment), the risk of subtle implausibility going unnoticed without deliberate expert review is not yet well quantified.
