# Phenomenon-specific cognitive maps omit follow-up investigative measures and non-technical case context

## Summary

The authors of the cognitive-map approach state this limitation directly: "we decided to omit follow-up investigative measures after having identified a suspect," and note that the complexity of real operational-level work may necessitate diving into a more granular, fully meshed interactive representation than the CM provides. The CM is therefore validated and useful only for the technical crime-scene portion of an investigation, not for what happens procedurally or legally once a suspect is identified.

## Why It Matters

An investigator who treats a phenomenon-specific cognitive map as a complete investigative plan risks stopping short at the point of suspect identification, missing the follow-up measures (arrest coordination, additional evidence gathering to support prosecution, asset seizure, etc.) that the map does not model. Because the CM is validated against literature and expert congruency only for its technical scope, its apparent thoroughness in mapping the digital crime scene can create a false impression of operational completeness.
