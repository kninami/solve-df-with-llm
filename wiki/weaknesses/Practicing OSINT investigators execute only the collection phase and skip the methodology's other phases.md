# Practicing OSINT investigators execute only the collection phase and skip the methodology's other phases

## Summary

Of 213 surveyed police students who had completed OSINT methodology training, only 3% reported completing all six phases of the methodology in their subsequent practical work, while 75.11% reported using only the Collection phase. Even among the subset who reported partially following the methodology, Collection dominated (75% used it) compared to Processing/Validation (22.53%), Analysis (17.84%), and Distribution and evaluation (10.79%).

## Why It Matters

Skipping the processing/validation and analysis phases means collected open-source information may be used in an investigation without formal verification of its reliability or systematic interpretation of what it means for the case, increasing the risk that unverified or low-quality open-source findings are acted upon. Skipping the distribution and evaluation phase similarly means findings may not be properly documented or communicated for use as evidence, potentially undermining the traceability and evidentiary value of otherwise sound OSINT collection work.
