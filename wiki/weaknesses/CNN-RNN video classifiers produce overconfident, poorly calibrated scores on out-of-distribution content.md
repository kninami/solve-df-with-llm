# CNN-RNN video classifiers produce overconfident, poorly calibrated scores on out-of-distribution content

## Summary

An evaluation of feedforward-CNN and CNN+LSTM deepfake detectors found a major, roughly equal-sized drop in detection performance for all model types and sizes when evaluated on videos generated by manipulation methods absent from training, and traced most of the accuracy loss to a shift in the optimal classification threshold: on out-of-sample positive instances, the models remained confident on negatives but produced a skewed, low-confidence distribution for positives, so classifying at the standard 0.5 threshold used in training substantially under-detects true positives. Applying image-perturbation training augmentation, expected to improve cross-manipulation robustness based on prior literature, did not reliably fix this in the study. Independently, a CNN+bidirectional-GRU pornography-video classifier was found to produce overconfident scores on out-of-distribution examples, which the authors addressed during training with label smoothing and score recalibration rather than treating the raw softmax output as a reliable confidence measure.

## Why It Matters

An investigator who deploys a CNN-RNN video classifier trained on a known manipulation-method or content distribution, and interprets its raw output score at face value against novel or out-of-distribution video, risks a materially higher false-negative rate than the model's published in-sample benchmarks suggest — deepfake or pornographic content generated by a method not represented in training can be systematically under-flagged rather than merely flagged with lower confidence. Because the failure mode is a threshold/calibration shift rather than a random accuracy drop, it is not obvious from the model's own score distribution alone, and training-data augmentation intended to address it (image perturbation) cannot be assumed to work without independent validation.
