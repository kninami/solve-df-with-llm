# First responders can inflate device-prioritisation scoresheet answers to increase seizure priority

## Summary

Because a device's assigned priority score influences how quickly it reaches examination, and examiners typically work through a backlog in priority order, a first responder has an incentive — whether to expedite their own case or simply out of optimism about a device's relevance — to answer scoresheet questions in a way that inflates the resulting score beyond what the device's actual evidential value warrants.

## Why It Matters

If inflated scoring is common or goes unchecked, the prioritisation system's core purpose — directing limited examination resources toward the devices most likely to matter — is undermined, and examiners may end up working through devices in an order that does not reflect genuine investigative priority. Because the scoresheet is intended to be an accountable, reviewable record, undetected inflation also risks misleading anyone later relying on the documented score as evidence of the device's assessed relevance at the time of seizure.
