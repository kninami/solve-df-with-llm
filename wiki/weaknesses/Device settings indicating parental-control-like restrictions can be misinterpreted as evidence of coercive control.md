# Device settings indicating parental-control-like restrictions can be misinterpreted as evidence of coercive control

## Summary

The paper cautions explicitly against over-interpreting the presence of any single setting: "it is not the intention of the author to sensationalise or speculate on how services may or may not be used as part of an inquiry... where relevant, the potential use of a setting or functionality is highlighted in order to stress possible intervention or investigative opportunities that could otherwise have been missed or remained unknown," and separately notes that "some settings like limits placed upon App usage may genuinely be deployed for purposes of productivity/prevention of procrastination and therefore it is important not to immediately judge the deployment of these settings as malicious without further scrutiny."

## Why It Matters

An investigator or first responder who treats the presence of a restrictive setting (e.g. Screen Time App Limits, Family Sharing location sharing, or Guided Access) as itself confirming coercive control risks over-attributing benign, self-directed, or genuinely protective configurations to an abusive actor, undermining the credibility of the finding; equally, dismissing such settings outright risks missing genuine abuse indicators, since the same settings genuinely are used by perpetrators of DCC.
